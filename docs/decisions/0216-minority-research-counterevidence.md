# 0216 — Retain minority research counterevidence as uncertainty

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Date:** 2026-11-05

## Context

The corrected Codex research pilot completes 47 host-observed native calls without a human wait. Its final independent decision still rejects the brief correctly: one retained statement has a documented exception. One verifier refutes its wording, while two confirm it with qualifications. The strict-majority dropping threshold is two, so the claim stays, but the shared report preserves its original high confidence and adds no open question for that conflict. Visible dissent in the examination list is insufficient when the main assertion still reads as established fact.

## Decision

A retained claim with any active refutation has low confidence, an explicit disputed label and an open question carrying the counterargument. The original claim text, citations, independent votes and configured dropping quorum remain unchanged. One refutation does not discard a claim; two confirmations do not erase a counterexample. The same pure projection serves standalone research and the unattended runner, so blocked versus complete-with-unknowns continues to follow the selected uncertainty policy.

This changes no public schema or authority. It does not manufacture a corrected factual assertion, an accepting source decision or a native campaign pass. Historical reports and decisions remain untouched; current eligibility and newly rendered projections remain separate from their original bytes.

## Alternatives rejected

- Dropping every singly refuted claim would weaken the configured strict-majority rule.
- Leaving confidence high because most votes confirm would hide unresolved counterevidence.
- Rewriting the assertion automatically would require an unsupported semantic judgment; preserve the disputed wording and its uncertainty instead.

## Verification

Six differential controls reproduce the defect across complete quorums of three, five, seven and nine and both runner uncertainty policies. A pure readback of the actual pilot's fourteen claims and 42 examinations exposes its disputed statement with low confidence and four open questions, while the original failed report checksum remains unchanged. A new native trial is still required to establish acceptance of the corrected core.
